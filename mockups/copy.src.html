part three · taking a note

What “take what you need” should actually do

Right now it opens a random note in an overlay and you close it again — nothing is really taken. Four alternatives, in rising order of how literal the taking is.

t1 · read it where it lives

you've survived every bad day drink water you are not behind
it's okay to rest. resting is still moving. — a stranger

The camera flies to a real note and enlarges it in place — no overlay, no dimming. You end up somewhere on the wall, next to its neighbours, free to wander from there. Taking becomes going.

t2 · it comes off in your hand

you've survived every bad day drink water
it's okay to rest. resting is still moving. — a stranger keep it  ·  put it back

The note peels off the wall with the same curl as your own peel, and hovers in front of you. Keep it saves it to your pocket; put it back returns it. The wall visibly still has it — nothing is removed from anyone else.

t3 · a pocket of notes you kept

you've survived every bad day drink water you are not behind
pocket · 3

Builds on T2: kept notes go into a small pocket in the corner, saved in your own browser only. Somewhere to return to on a bad night without hunting the wall. Nothing leaves your device; nobody sees what you kept.

t4 · one note, chosen for you

someone out here is glad you're still going. — a stranger

another  ·  go to it  ·  keep it

Deliberately ceremonial — the wall recedes entirely and you're handed exactly one note, alone on the screen. Closest to today, but slowed down and given weight. Best if “take what you need” is meant to feel like being handed something.
