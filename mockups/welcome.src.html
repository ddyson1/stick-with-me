How the wall greets you

All of these keep the wall on screen from the first second and put the introduction into the world rather than above it. They differ in how much they say, and in what kind of object says it.

w1 · the origin note

you've survived every bad day so far it's okay to rest drink water. text them back. you are not behind
stick with me a wall of support from strangers, for strangers.

take what you need.
leave what you can. take one  ·  leave one
+−⌂
0, 0

One white note at the origin, in the same hand as everything else. Quietest. Risk: a newcomer may read it as just another stranger's note rather than the house speaking.

w2 · the note that points

you've survived every bad day so far drink water. text them back. you are not behind
stick with me a wall of support from strangers, for strangers.

take what you need — tap any note.
leave what you can — pull a sheet ↘
+−⌂
0, 0

The same note, but it teaches by pointing — a hand-drawn dashed arrow curls from the note down to the pad. Uses the doodle language the site already has. Warmest and the clearest about what to do.

w3 · the index card

you've survived every bad day so far it's okay to rest drink water. text them back. you are not behind
the house stick with me a wall of support from strangers, for strangers. take what you need; leave what you can. take one  ·  leave one  ·  the rules
+−⌂
0, 0

A ruled index card taped to the wall — a different object from a sticky, so it reads unmistakably as the house speaking, not a stranger. Room for a rules link. Slightly more formal.

w4 · three notes, in someone's hand

you've survived every bad day so far you are not behind
stick with me a wall of support from strangers, for strangers.
take what you need —
tap any note here.
leave what you can —
pull a sheet from the pad.
+−⌂
0, 0

The welcome is three notes arranged like someone laid them out for you — name, then each half of the ethic, each on its own paper. Teaches the whole idea without a paragraph, and shows off colour immediately. Takes more of the wall.

w5 · written on the wall itself

stick with me

a wall of support from strangers, for strangers.
take what you need · leave what you can

you've survived every bad day so far it's okay to rest drink water. text them back. you are not behind
+−⌂
0, 0

No object at all — the words are painted faintly on the plaster, behind every note. Nothing to dismiss, nothing to peel, never in the way; notes stuck over it partially cover it, which is rather lovely as the wall fills. Boldest and most restrained. Weakest at telling you what to do.
