part one · the editor

Four ways to make a note

Today's editor is a white card with a tool column — it works, but it reads like a settings panel bolted to a sticky. These push in different directions: toward objects, toward nothing, or toward tighter chrome.

E1 · the desk — tools as objects

you don't have to be okay today— a stranger

paper

pen

hand · doodle

Aa Aa Aa ✎

e1

A felt desk. Paper is a stack of sheets you fan through, ink is a row of markers (the taller one is uncapped — the one you're using), and the doodle toggle sits with the hands. Nothing looks like a form control. Warmest, most in keeping with everything else we've built.

E2 · bare — the note is the whole interface

someone out here is glad you're still going— a stranger
Aa ✎

e2

No card at all — the note floats big on the dimmed wall with one pill of tools beneath it. Fewest pixels, most focus on the words. “Toss” moves into a small × on the note corner. Cleanest, but the tools are less discoverable.

E3 · compact — toolbar welded to the note

small steps count. tiny ones. crumbs.
Aa ✎

e3

The tools become the bottom edge of the note itself — one object, no floating panel. Most compact and the least redesign from what exists. Safe, tidy, a bit software-ish.

E4 · margins — tools tucked into the note's edges

ask for help. it's a skill, not a defeat.
Aa Aa Aa ✎

e4

Paper colours become tabs on the top edge, inks a pen tray down the left, hands a small strip at the bottom. The note is literally the only object on screen. Most inventive; also the fiddliest on a phone.

part three · the default screen

What people see first

Right now a tall hero pushes the wall below the fold — you have to scroll to find the thing itself. All three of these put the wall on screen immediately.

D1 · wall first — chrome shrinks to one floating bar

stick with me take one leave one
you've survived every bad day so far it's okay to rest drink water. text them back. you are not behind

d1

You land inside the wall. Title and both actions live in a single frosted pill at the top. Everything else — pad, zoom, coordinates — stays where it is. Most direct; the wall sells itself.

D2 · the welcome note — the wall explains itself

you've survived every bad day so far it's okay to rest drink water. text them back. you are not behind
stick with me a wall of support from strangers, for strangers. take what you need. leave what you can. → take one  ·  leave one

d2

The introduction is a note on the wall, pinned at the origin — same paper, same handwriting, same shadows as everything else. No chrome at all. Wanders away as you explore and comes back via the ⌂ button. The most charming; the most confident.

D3 · split — a reading rail beside the wall

d3

A permanent rail on the left holds the name, the two actions and the live count; the wall runs beside it. Best for explaining the project to newcomers, but it permanently eats a third of the wall and feels more like an app.
